The problem

A quadcopter has to hold its perch in wind. Quantify how gusts load it across every wind angle, and find when it slides, tips, or lifts off.

My role

Ran a CFD wind-load study in Ansys Fluent across a sweep of wind angles, deriving force- and moment-balance equations for sliding, tipping, and lift-off to compute the critical wind speed - and validated Reynolds independence from 9-54 m/s.

Outcome

Found a worst-case critical speed of 35 mph, then designed a perch shield in SolidWorks that cut drone drag by up to ~47% and added friction to resist sliding - raising the critical wind speed in the worst-case direction to 88 mph.

A sample from the logging loop
weather_station.py PYTHON 
import time
import board
import adafruit_bme280.advanced as bme280

# BME280 over I2C: temperature, pressure, humidity
i2c = board.I2C()
sensor = bme280.Adafruit_BME280_I2C(i2c, address=0x77)
sensor.sea_level_pressure = 1013.25

with open("weather_log.csv", "a") as log:
    while True:
        stamp = time.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")
        t = sensor.temperature          # deg C
        p = sensor.pressure             # hPa
        h = sensor.relative_humidity    # %
        log.write(f"{stamp},{t:.2f},{p:.2f},{h:.2f}\n")
        log.flush()
        print(f"{stamp}  {t:.1f} C  {p:.1f} hPa  {h:.1f} %")
        time.sleep(60)

The problem

Aids for blind and low-vision users - canes, guide dogs, another person - can struggle with moving objects, cost, or independence, and vision loss rises with age. The goal: an affordable, easy-to-use wearable that helps someone sense the obstacles around them.

My role

A four-person team project. I led the CAD, designing the cap-mounted enclosure; helped assemble the hardware; shaped the concept (mission, goals, and design direction); and did much of the presenting.

How it works

HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors (range ~2-400 cm) feed distance to an Arduino, which drives vibration motors whose intensity rises as objects get closer, across three detection directions. It clips onto a cap, works alongside a cane, and pops off for hygiene.

Outcome

User-tested: averaged 6.8/10 for comfort (up to 8/10), best accuracy out to ~13 feet, and rated intuitive and easy to use.

The brief

A client brief from the National Recreation & Park Association: design, model, and test an automated device to separate recyclable materials - no water separation, built on one base plate with one custom part, sorting in under two minutes. Recyclables were represented by same-size balls of different materials, to be routed into separate bins.

My role

Led a team of three through the full design process on a one-month timeline: three initial concepts scored in a decision matrix, then a coding flowchart and design sketch, then the build.

How it works

A line-follower sensor identifies each ball, with start and stop buttons for control. A motor feeds the balls through one at a time; one servo acts like a toll gate to release them, and a second rotates the bin to drop each ball in the right slot.

Outcome

Sorted everything in 1:17, well under the two-minute limit - the only team of about 24 in the school to get a working device. Presented to a panel of four clients.
